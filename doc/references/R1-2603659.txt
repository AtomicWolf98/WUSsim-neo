B21: Power  model
B22: RRM measurement by EE processing 
B23: Intra-frequency: measurement only by EE processing -synchronous case
B24: For  measurement  only by EE processing, there is no cell  search  and known neighboring cells are assumed.  C ompared with serving cell measurement, intra-frequency neighboring cell measurement involves more cells to measure and potential timing difference between serving cell and neighboring cell, which may requires additional time hypothesis to search for the SSB of the neighboring cell.  Both the increased number of cells and the additional number of time hypothesis impact the UE complexity and power consumption. Therefore, it ’ s better to limit both numbers.
B25: The number of neighboring cells
B26: Assume the  number  of known neighboring cells to be measured is N, and  for each known cell, two sequences (1 PSS and 1 SSS) are to be detected per time hypothesis. Therefore, there will be a  total  of 2*N sequence correlations per time hypothesis. In NR, power model for both N= 4 and 8 neighboring cells are available. Both N=4 and 8 can be also considered for EE processing. 
B27: The timing difference between serving cell and neighboring cell
B28: Given that UE can perform time and frequency calibration in serving cell, e.g., up to [2us] residual time error, and up to [5ppm] residual frequency error, and both time and frequency calibration results can apply to the neighboring cell. Then, the number of additional time  hypothsis  required for detecting the neighboring cell SSB depends on the  timing difference between serving cell and neighboring cell .  For synchronous case, actual SSB transmissions from cells are   assumed   time-aligned , and thus, the time difference between serving cell and neighboring cell SSB with same index comprises of  base station timing difference, e.g., 3us for TDD, and  propagation  delay, e.g., 1.6us for 500m ISD and 4.8us for 1500m ISD.  Consequently, the  additional   number   time hypothesis is determined by ( time difference/ time sample duration) and the time difference is limited for synchronous case, i.e. serval micro seconds.
B29: Additionally , the EE processing power for WUS detection has been  agreed  in  previous  meetings, which considers a number of sequence detections per time hypothesis and per frequency hypothesis, and a number of time hypothesis and a number of frequency hypothesis with the assumption of   up to [2us] residual time error, and up to [5ppm] residual frequency error .

B81: DL WUS operation
B82: I n  NR   Rel-18 and  Rel-19,  NR LP-WUS is applicable to use cases such as IoT, wearables and  eMBB . In 6GR,  both  eMBB  and IoT  devices desire high UE energy efficiency   and thus,  DL WUS  operation can be studied for both of them . To streamline  network operations and specification effort , a  unified  DL WUS procedure design is recommended  f or both  eMBB  and IoT  use cases .

B239: Impact on the EE processing complexity
B240: C ompare d to serving cell RRM measurement  by EE processing ,  higher complexity is expected for  neighbor cell RRM measurement,  considering  cell identification /detection , measurement  over multiple cells   to be performed by EE processing .  In this section, the number of sequence correlations is used to  facilitate analyzing  the  additional  EE processing complexity. 
B241: As  discussed in section 2.2.1, for  intra-frequency measurement only by EE processing   in   synchronous case ,  the number of sequence correlations per time hypothesis  will be  a total of 2*N sequence correlations per time hypothesis ,  where N is the number of cells for  measurement . Considering  the number of  time hypothesis  is  limited for synchronous case ,  the UE complexity for  intra-frequency measurement only  with  N =4 and 8   by EE processing   is  comparable to  WUS detection .
B242: Observation  12 :  The EE processing complexity of intra-frequency measurement only by EE processing in synchronous case is  comparable to  WUS detection .
B243: For  intra-frequency  measurement and search by EE  processing   in   synchronous case , cell search is also performed by EE processing.  Similarly, the time difference between serving cell and neighboring cell SSB with same index comprises of  base station timing difference, e.g., 3us for TDD, and propagation delay, e.g., 1.6us for 500m ISD and 4.8us for 1500m ISD, and thus, time hypothesis within a time window of only several micro seconds are performed for the cell search.  However, if the  neighboring cell ID  is unknown, the  number of sequence correlations   per time hypothesis  would be large.  To reduce the complexity and power consumption of cell search, assisted information, i.e., a list of neighboring cell ID can be provided to the UE,  among which cell search is performed by EE processing .
B244: For inter -frequency  measurement   by EE  processing ,  w hen cell search is performed for a frequency layer, time and frequency synchronization can be achieved, and thus, after cell search, the measurement operation in the detected frequency layer is similar to the synchronous case of intra-frequency cell measurement, and the EE processing complexity is comparable under the assumed same number of cells.
B245: Observation  13 : With p rovided list of neighboring cell IDs  and a limited time window for cell search, the EE processing complexity of intra-frequency measurement and cell search  can be  reduced .  
B246: Observation  14 :  T he  EE  processing  complexity  of  one frequency layer for  inter-frequency measurement  only  is comparable to in tra-frequency cell measurement   under the assumed same number of cells .  
B247: Proposal  16 : For i ntra-frequency   measurement and cell search by EE processing ,  study providing  a list  of neighboring cell IDs  and  limiting the  time window   for cell search  to re duce  the EE processing complexity.
B248: For asynchronous  intra-frequency   case  and inter-frequency case, there  exists  time uncertainty  in  the SMTC window, assume 2ms or 5ms SMTC duration, it may involve  certain  number of time hypothesis, and thus, it may  cause high  EE processing complexity . B319: Agreement @RAN1#12 3  meeting For FTP Model 3, the packet delay budget (PDB) can be additionally considered, The latency characteristic of the traffic in RAN side (i.e., air interface) is modelled as packet delay budget (PDB). The PDB is a limited time budget for a packet to be transmitted over the air from a BS to a UE for DL, or from a UE to a BS for UL.  For a given packet, the delay of the packet incurred in air interface is measured from the time that the packet arrives at the BS to the time that it is successfully transferred to the UE for DL, or from a UE to a BS for UL. If the delay is larger than a given PDB for the packet, the packet is said to violate PDB, otherwise the packet is said to be successfully delivered. Values for PDB, e.g., {10ms, 20ms, 30ms, 50ms, 100ms, 200ms, 300ms, 1000ms, 2000ms} can be considered. Which values will be used will consider the use case for the evaluations.

B367: Network complexity 
B368: For network complexity, we think both DL WUS operation with and without C-DRX does not cause much network complexity, since both are  builted  based on NR Rel-19 LP-WUS operation option 1-2, with DL WUS with C-DRX being supported as a special configuration of DL WUS without C-DRX.

B370: From scheduling perspective, DL WUS without C-DRX removes periodicity constraints and lets the scheduler operate in a demand-based way, which may actually simplify scheduling for the mixed,  aperiodic, or delay-sensitive traffic .

B386: Decoupling L1/L3 measurements from C-DRX cycle
B387: In NR, the current design of some L1/L3 measurements and reporting procedures is coupled with the C-DRX cycle.  For example, as shown in Table 8.1.2.2-1 and Table 8.1.2.4-1 in 3GPP TS 38.133 that the out-of-sync evaluation duration for RLM without DRX configuration is shorter, whereas RLM with DRX configuration allows a longer duration for declaring out-of-sync. In other words, DRX-based RLM requirements are more relaxed, helping reduce UE power consumption.  Besides, similar restriction is defined in RRM requirements as specified in TS 38.133, i.e. RRM requirements is coupled with C-DRX cycle. This creates a constraint where the measurement behavior is tightly coupled with the DRX configuration. However, in practice, it is well understood that the configuration of the C-DRX cycle is primarily driven by service characteristics, such as traffic periodicity and latency requirements. In contrast, the periodicity of RRM or RLM/BFD measurements should be determined by the UE mobility characteristics and the actual network deployment scenarios. Therefore, these two aspects are fundamentally driven by different design considerations and do not have an intrinsic dependency. From a design perspective, it would be more appropriate to decouple them from 6G Day-1 design, rather than inheriting the coupling from existing systems.
B388: Observation  25 :  In 5G NR design, the periodicity of RRM/RLM/BFD measurements is tied to the C-DRX cycle. However, since the two are driven by fundamentally different considerations and have no intrinsic relationship, they should be decoupled in the 6G Day-1 design.
B389: Therefore, it is necessary to design a measurement and reporting mechanism that decouples UE measurements and reports from the DRX cycle, in order to fully exploit the benefits of  DL  WUS operation in 6GR , especially when it operates without C-DRX . The below  F igure  6  provides an illustrative example.   In particular, for L1 measurements, such as periodic CSI and L1-RSRP measurements and reporting, the current design only allows measurements and reports to be performed during the C-DRX active time. Furthermore, under the Rel-19 LP-WUS option 1-2, if the higher-layer parameter  lpwus-TransmitOtherPeriodicCSI  is configured, the UE is still required to report periodic CSI and L1-RSRP during the time defined by the configured  drx-onDurationTimer  in DRX configuration, even when the UE is not indicated to wake up by LP-WUS. This results in unnecessary UE power consumption. For 6GR  DL  WUS operation , especially when it operates without C-DRX , periodic CSI and L1-RSRP measurements and reporting can be designed to better align with the UE’s actual activity, thereby improving power efficiency. B427: Necessity ,  Compelxity  and  Feasibility for  RLM  by EE processing in RRC connected
B428: 
B429: In the last meeting,  RLM by EE processing  was proposed but not agreed. From our understanding, the  uncertainty  seems mainly from the feasibility perspective, e.g.,  the metric for RLM is SINR  which  is  different  from RRM  of  RSRP and/or  RLM is related to PDCCH failure   should be more reliable  etc   [3] .  We agree that t he metrics and requirements are different  for RLM and RRM by EE processing, hence   the feasibility of RLM  by EE processing  needs to be  studied and  checked separately  from RRM by EE processing , including SINR/SNR derivation, required number of samples, reliability of Qin/ Qout  discrimination  etc .  In the following, we first  provide our views on the necessity  on  study  RLM  by EE processing , and then we  discuss  the feasibility and provide our  initial  evaluation result in terms of SINR accuracy and required number of measurement samples for  RLM  by EE processing . 
B430: Necessity
B431: For RRC connected   UE power saving, the overall benefit of EE  processing  based measurement  for serving cell  should be considered from the complete set of mandatory serving   cell  measurement  operations, rather than from RRM measurement alone.  I f EE processing is introduced only for serving   cell RRM measurement while serving   cell RLM is still periodically performed by non-EE processing,  then  the overall UE power saving gain may be limited, since the  periodicity for RLM and RRM for serving cell is similar, the  periodic wake-up of non-EE processing for RLM  would  dominate or significantly reduce the benefit obtained from EE-based RRM measurement.   As observed in above section of p ower saving benefits for RRM/RLM measurement by EE  processing vs non-EE processing  for serving cell only, if RLM is  performed  by non-EE processing,  the potential 35.55%~ 69.58 % measurement   related power saving gain from EE processing  can not be fully achieved.   Therefore, once EE  processing  based measurement  for serving cell  is considered for RRC  connected mode , it is necessary to also study whether  and how  serving   cell RLM measurement can be assisted or performed by EE processing. 
B432: O bservation   27 :   For RRC connected mode, i f serving cell measurement by EE processing is introduced only for RRM measurement, while RLM still requires periodic non-EE processing, the UE  CANNOT   fully  obtain  the potential 35.55%~69.58% measurement related power saving gain from EE processing .  
B433: Proposal   26 :  T he study of  serving cell measurement by EE processing for RRC connected mode  should include RLM  in addition to RRM  measurement  to obtain the  promising  overall power saving gain , while separately evaluating  RLM  feasibility due to the different metric and reliability requirements from RRM measurement.
B434: Complexity
B435: Compared with neighbor-cell measurement, RLM  by EE processing  is operationally simpler  since  serving-cell RLM does not require blind cell search, PCI detection, or inter-frequency discovery. The RLM reference resources are configured by the network, and the UE is already synchronized to the serving cell . 
B436: Observation   28 :  Serving   cell RLM  by EE processing is  simpler than  neighbour   cell measurement  by EE processing , since the UE is already synchronized and the RLM resources are configured.

B456: For  paging reception in RRC idle state,  more than one  SSB samples  may be  required  to facilitate  AGC  and  synchronization. With DL WUS, U E   can   potentially  sleep longer  and  deeper ,  which  may results in a loss of accurate  time-frequency synchronization . If multiple SSB samples required for paging reception are from multiple SSB periods,  significant  delay for PO reception could occur,  especially  for large SSB periodicity. To reduce latency,  on-demand SSB/RS  should  be studied.  Two scenarios are considered as listed below and  issustrated  in  Figure  8   and  Figure  9 :  
B457: Scenario 1:  There is only one  SSB burst  per  SSB period .  In  such s cenario, on-demand SSB/RS can  help   to get UE ready for paging reception within one SSB period instead of  across multiple SSB  peirod s  to  reduce  latency . 
B458: Scenario 2:  There are several  SSB bursts  with repetitions per  SSB period .  In  such  s cenairo , although there are SSB bursts  with repetitions  within one SSB period, depending on WUS   MO locations, it may  not  be enough  for  paging reception   once  triggered by DL WUS,  on-demand SSB/RS can  help to provide additional samples within the same SSB period. 
B459: For the two scenarios above, t he on-demand SSB/RS  can be  transmitted only  if  network is going to page UE(s)  and  and  transmits WUS. When UE detects WUS successfully, it can know on-demand SSB/RS is transmitted .

B463: WUS triggering on demand Cell-DTX/DRX active time
B464: Cell-DTX/DRX was introduced in NR for Network Energy Saving (NES), allowing UEs to use periodic active and inactive  periods  managed via DCI format 2_9 or RRC  signalling . However, DCI format 2_9 only  activat es /deactivate s  these  Cell on-off  patterns during UE active  time  and  cannot trigger  additional  active duration during the cell non-active period  based on traffic demand. Also, UEs don't monitor WUS during  Cell  DTX inactiv e time in NR , limiting responsive wake-up capability  although the power consumption for the UE to monitor LP-WUS is low . To improve scheduling flexibility  and reducing the traffic  deliervy  delay , 6GR should enable WUS monitoring during DTX inactive periods, letting the network trigger  UE or Cell  activity dynamically as needed without increasing power  consumption.
B465: Proposal  29 :  6GR should  study  supporting WUS monitoring during  Cell  DTX inactiv e period  to  trigger  on-demand cell DTX  active period , improving  scheduling flexibility and latency  while maintaining low power consumption.

B467: WUS directly triggers  RACH  transmission  
B468: I n NR RRC idle state, LP-WUS is applied to trigger paging PDCCH monitoring and the UE can be in ultra-deep sleep or deep sleep to save power unless the UE successfully detects its targeted LP-WUS. As LP-WUS would wake up the corresponding subgroup, the finer the indication granularity, the lower false wake-up and thus, larger power saving gain can be achieved. As an extreme case, UE-specific wake-up indication would be best for UE power saving. When such WUS function is studied, FAR should be comparable to CRC FAR of PDCCH, i.e., 10-7 level.

B519: Observation  17 :
B520: C-DRX, NR Rel-16 DCP, and DL WUS with 160ms C-DRX cycle show flatter CDFs with longer tails, indicating a larger delay spread across UEs and a greater gap between better-delay and worse-delay UEs.
B521: C-DRX, NR Rel-16 DCP, and DL WUS with 40ms C-DRX cycle show steep CDFs, indicating that reducing the C-DRX cycle to  40  ms  can improve the packet delay distribution.
B522: Among the considered schemes, DL WUS without C-DRX provides the most concentrated packet delay distribution and the best packet delay performance across UEs. 
B523: Observation   18 : For the C-DRX (160-8-100) case, DL WUS operation without C-DRX is the only evaluated solution that can simultaneously achieve significant UE power saving gain  i.e., 22.67% for the light sleep scheme and 57.21% for the deep sleep scheme  and significant packet delay improvement  i.e., 65.94%/53.27% average latency gain and 81.41%/75.98% latency gain at 90% percentile for the light/deep sleep schemes, respectively , while NR DCP and DL WUS with C-DRX provide only limited UE power saving gain  i.e., 3.71% and 5.62%, respectively  and degrade packet delay performance.
B524: Observation   19 :  For   delay sensitive traffic with  PDB  requirement is around  30  ms , with  C-DRX  of  (40-4-25) ,    all  the evaluated schemes  such as C-DRX only,  NR DCP and DL WUS with C-DRX , DL WUS without C-DRX can meet the delay requirement from the average-latency perspective. However, from 90%- ile  packet latency perspective, C-DRX only, NR DCP and DL WUS with C-DRX does not meet the PDB requirement, while DL WUS without C-DRX can meet the PDB requirement for both light and deep sleep cases. Therefore,  DL WUS operation without C-DRX provides the highest UE power saving gain among all evaluated schemes  while meeting the traffic PDB requirement , and is therefore the most power-efficient solution.
